The why

What’s Driving The Path

Why Physiotherapy And Performance Coaching?

I'm genuinely fascinated by anatomy, by sport, and by what it takes to help someone rebuild and grow them into the best possible version of themselves as an athlete. That interest isn't just theoretical — throughout my teenage years playing, I picked up injury after injury and became a regular face in the physio's chair. Being on that side of the table so often is what showed me I don't just want to treat an injury and hand an athlete back to their coach. I want to be part of both ends — the clinical work that gets someone right, and the training work that gets them better than they were before.

Why This Pathway?

I chose to build the science degree first so I'd have the full picture — anatomy, physiology, biomechanics — before specialising in either direction. ASCA Level 1 comes next so I can start coaching real athletes while I'm still studying, instead of waiting until I'm fully qualified to get in the room. The Master's in High Performance Sport & Physiotherapy is what lets me actually practice clinically at the level I want to work at — pitch-side, with elite athletes.

Before the classroom

National Athletics, then the NPL system

Before I set my sights on physiotherapy and coaching, I competed as a national-level athlete in athletics for a number of years — track and field gave me an early, firsthand look at what serious training and competition actually demands. I later moved into football, playing in the NPL (National Premier Leagues) system here in Sydney, where I saw up close what separates athletes who come back well from injury from those who don't. Between the two, that's a big part of why I'm building toward a career that sits right at that intersection.
